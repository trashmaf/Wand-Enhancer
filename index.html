Prevention

Six habits that keep the bao at bay

Wash your hands

20 seconds with soap. Bun flour is sneakier than you think.

Keep your distance

Stay at least two steamer baskets away from any open dim sum cart.

Mask up near steam

Steam carries the aroma. A mask cuts craving intensity by a made-up 80%.

Stay hydrated

Thirst often disguises itself as a bun craving. Drink water first.

Sleep 8 hours

Tired brains negotiate badly with pork belly fillings.

Get the booster

One balanced meal a day keeps your bao immunity topped up.

How it spreads

Mostly through the nose

  1. Steam release. A fresh basket opens and aroma particles rise.
  2. Airborne drift. Those particles travel up to 12 metres down a food court.
  3. Inhalation. One sniff and the craving sets in.
  4. Social spread. Saying "want to split some bao?" infects everyone at your table.

FAQ

Common questions

Is the Bao Virus real?

No. This site is a parody. For real health guidance, go to your local public health authority.

What should I do if I test positive?

Eat a bao. Symptoms usually clear up in about four bites.

Are vegetarian buns safer?

Not really. Variant X-7 has been seen in red bean, custard and mushroom buns too.

Can pets catch it?

Dogs are extremely susceptible. Keep buns off low tables.
